# Limitations and Research Boundaries

EdgeTruth has been robustly evaluated against several threat models, but we recognize specific limitations and define the boundaries of our claims:

1. **Fresh Identity Optimistic Initialization**: New identities currently enter the system with an optimistic prior ($P_i = 1.0$). While malicious nodes cannot extract surplus from Win-Then-Drop attacks (due to contingent payment), an attacker could theoretically farm initial allocations by constantly creating new identities to execute tasks honestly, bypassing capacity limits or shedding accumulated negative reputation.
2. **Possible Reputation Farming**: A node could perform honestly on low-value tasks to build a strong reliability posterior, then exploit it to secure a high-value task and drop it.
3. **Price Inflation by Highly Reliable Nodes**: Because the scoring function heavily penalizes failure risk for high-value tasks, highly reliable nodes gain significant market power. They can inflate their price bids to extract the surplus generated by their reliability, leaving the buyer with lower direct utility compared to a naive greedy allocator.
4. **Strategic Price Bidding**: EdgeTruth uses a first-price contingent rule. While it incentivizes reliable execution, the price bidding remains strategic. It is not fully incentive compatible on the price dimension.
5. **Empirical Collusion Guarantees**: Our resistance to collusion is empirically supported against the tested 2-, 3-, and 5-node collusion strategies (e.g., High-Low pricing). We do not claim universal mathematical collusion-proofness across all possible ring topologies.
6. **Empirical Sybil Guarantees**: Our resistance to Sybil attacks is similarly bounded to the tested Win-Then-Drop adversarial behaviors. We do not claim universal Sybil-proofness.
7. **No Runtime Benchmark Claim**: While the allocation step is theoretically $O(N)$, we have not performed or claimed formal microsecond-level runtime benchmarking (e.g., "2.5 ms").
8. **Evaluation Bounds**: All welfare, SLA, and regret results are bounded to the evaluated workloads (derived from Azure 2019 traces) and the specified threat models.
